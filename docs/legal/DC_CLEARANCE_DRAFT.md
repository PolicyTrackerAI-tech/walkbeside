## 1. Question and position

May Honest Funeral Co. run its free family service and its hospice-paid
program for DC families, including sending price-information requests to
DC funeral homes, without a license under the District of Columbia Funeral
Services Regulatory Act of 1984 (D.C. Code § 3-401 et seq.)?

**Position: yes, the strongest of the three.**
- DC's "practice of funeral directing" is anchored to custody. It is the
  care, disposal, and preserving of human remains. It has no
  "arrangements" clause like Virginia's and no "compensation to arrange"
  clause like Maryland's.
- DC's solicitation definition expressly excludes "responses to requests
  for information from consumers."
- DC's regulator and Attorney General have spent a decade forcing funeral
  prices into the open: an Attorney General survey of every DC home, a
  $300,000 settlement against a home, and a rule requiring online price
  lists.

There is one textual soft spot (§4.1). The real exposure is the CPPA (§6).

## 2. The statutory and regulatory framework

- **D.C. Code § 3-402 (definitions):**
  - "Practice of funeral directing" means "engaging in the care and
    disposal of human remains or the preserving by embalming or otherwise
    of human remains for transportation, funeral services, burial, or
    cremation." **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]**
  - "Funeral director" means a person licensed by the District to perform
    the practice of funeral directing, and includes "mortician,"
    "undertaker," and "embalmer." **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]**
  - **"Solicitation"** means any annoying or unseemly conduct by a
    **licensee, his employees, or agents**. The listed examples are: (A)
    loitering around hospitals, sanitariums, or personal care homes to
    solicit employment of the licensee's services; (B) offering, giving, or
    promising any gratuity or payment to any person for information
    concerning human remains; (C) requesting or recommending that a
    consumer change from another funeral services establishment; (D)
    disputing possession of remains; (E) initiating contact with the next
    of kin, relations, friends, or associates of the deceased to provide
    funeral services without being contacted by the next of kin or their
    representative. **"The term 'solicitation' shall not include general
    advertising, the sale of burial insurance, or responses to requests for
    information from consumers."** **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]**
- **§ 3-401 (purposes):** licensure and regulation of funeral directors,
  apprentices, and establishments, and protecting the public "from
  fraudulent, unfair, and deceptive practices by persons licensed to
  provide funeral directing services." **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]** The Act is
  aimed at licensees.
- **§ 3-411 (prohibited acts):** "No person shall operate a funeral services
  establishment in the District unless the person is licensed." An
  establishment licensee may not let anyone practice funeral directing
  unless that person is a licensed funeral director or a supervised
  apprentice. Licensees may not let others use their license.
  **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]**
- **§ 3-421 (services requiring immediate supervision of an apprentice):**
  an apprentice may not, without a funeral director's immediate
  supervision, handle, prepare, or embalm remains; remove or transport
  them; conduct or direct a funeral; or perform "**advising consumers
  making arrangements for the care and disposition of human remains,
  including arrangements made prior to the death of a person**."
  **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT via summary]** (See soft spot §4.1.)
- **§ 3-415 (courtesy cards for Maryland and Virginia funeral directors):**
  DC's scheme assumes a three-jurisdiction market, with homes crossing the
  lines routinely. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT, title]**
- **The DC Consumer Bill of Rights for Funeral Home Establishments
  (DLCP):** a funeral home "must post all of its price lists on any
  websites that it maintains," meaning the general price list, the casket
  price list, and the outer burial container price list. Homes must also
  notify consumers of their rights, including the right to choose
  individual goods and services instead of a preselected package.
  **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT via OAG release + DLCP page; pull the DCMR cite]**
- **17 DCMR chapters 30, 31, and 33** (funeral directors; funeral services
  establishments; general rules). Unembalmed remains held more than 24
  hours must be refrigerated (§ 3110.4). This is already verified in
  `docs/STATE_BODY_CARE_FINDINGS.md`.

## 3. Why the platform sits outside the licensed practice

1. **The defined practice is hands-on custody:** care, disposal, and
   preserving of remains. The platform never touches remains, never takes
   custody, and never directs a funeral. The Act's stated purpose (§ 3-401)
   is protecting the public from *licensees*.
2. **The solicitation carve-out describes our outreach.** "Responses to
   requests for information from consumers" are not solicitation. A
   family-authorized price-list request is a consumer's request for
   information. A DC home that answers it is doing the one thing DC's
   definition expressly blesses. The platform never initiates contact with
   next of kin (clause E). It pays nobody "for information concerning human
   remains" (clause B, which is also guardrail #1). It never recommends
   switching away from a home the family already chose (clause C; the
   neutral-presentation rule covers this).
3. **The District's own policy is our product.** The Office of the Attorney
   General:
   - surveyed the prices of all 38 DC funeral homes in 2017 and published
     the results (for example, embalming ran $375 to $995 with a $750
     average);
   - sent warning letters to 9 homes over pricing and sales practices;
   - publicly called on 6 homes to post their prices online;
   - obtained a $300,000 settlement from Austin Royster Funeral Home for
     deceiving grieving families about costs;
   - led a 2020 multistate push for the FTC to strengthen the Funeral Rule;
     and
   - proposed the Consumer Bill of Rights that now mandates online price
     lists.
   **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT: oag.dc.gov releases]** A regulator that surveys every
   home's prices and publishes the ranges (whether the 2017 PDF names homes
   individually was not confirmed in this pass) cannot coherently treat
   publishing and explaining funeral prices as unlicensed funeral directing.
4. **Price publishers operate openly in DC.** Consumers' Checkbook rates 49
   Washington-area homes. Its direct-cremation prices ranged $995 to $7,495
   (average $3,129), and its sample traditional funeral ranged $6,165 to
   $19,695. FCAME's survey covers DC homes. **[SECONDARY]**

## 4. The soft spot

### 4.1 § 3-421: "advising consumers making arrangements … including arrangements made prior to the death"

§ 3-421 lists "advising consumers making arrangements for the care and
disposition of human remains, including arrangements made prior to the
death of a person" among the tasks an **apprentice** may not do without a
funeral director's immediate supervision. A motivated reader could say this
shows advising arranging consumers, including pre-death, is part of licensed
funeral directing.

**Counters:** § 3-421 is an apprentice-supervision rule inside licensed
establishments. It does not define the practice; § 3-402 does, and § 3-402
is custody-anchored. Reading § 3-421 as a definition would make licensed
practice out of the Attorney General's own consumer alerts, FCA
volunteers, clergy, and hospice social workers.

**Mitigation:** DC-specific shaping of product copy. We advise families
**about prices and their rights**. We do not "advise on arrangements" in
the sense of recommending particular goods, services, or homes. The
neutral-presentation rule already keeps us from recommending a home.
**[COUNSEL: confirm; low priority relative to the Virginia questions.]**

## 5. Right of disposition

The family (next of kin or designee) makes every arrangement. The platform
is never a designee. As in Maryland §5, the authorization text says the
family authorized a **price-information request** only.

## 6. The real DC exposure: the Consumer Protection Procedures Act

DC's CPPA (D.C. Code § 28-3901 et seq.) reaches us squarely, even though
families pay nothing:

- **"Consumer"** includes a person who "does or would purchase, lease
  (from), or **receive** consumer goods or services." **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]**
  Receiving a free service is enough.
- **"Merchant"** includes a person "whether organized or operating for
  profit or for a nonprofit purpose, who in the ordinary course of business
  does or would sell, lease (to), or **transfer, either directly or
  indirectly**, consumer goods or services," or who supplies the goods or
  services that are the subject of a trade practice. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]**
- **"Trade practice"** means any act which does or would "create, alter,
  repair, furnish, make available, **provide information about**, or,
  directly or indirectly, solicit or offer for or effectuate, a sale, lease
  or transfer, of consumer goods or services." **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]** Our
  entire product provides information about the sale of funeral goods and
  services. Every public claim we make is a trade practice.
- **Remedies:** treble damages or **$1,500 per violation**, whichever is
  greater, plus fees. **[SECONDARY via multiple sources]**
- **Who can sue:** individual consumers, and **public-interest
  organizations** suing on behalf of consumers with only a "sufficient
  nexus" to their interests (§ 28-3905(k)(1)(D); Article III standing not
  required in DC Superior Court). DC courts have endorsed this broad
  organizational standing, and public-interest-group CPPA suits against
  businesses are common. **[SECONDARY via Hunton, WLF, Inside Class
  Actions]**
- **No reliance or intent** is required for the core misrepresentation and
  omission prongs of § 28-3904. **[SECONDARY; COUNSEL confirm]**

**What this means in practice.** Every load-bearing claim must be
substantiated before a DC audience sees it:

1. **"Free to families, forever."** If any feature is ever gated on a
   hospice code (counsel packet Q5), this claim becomes a CPPA
   misrepresentation risk in DC first.
2. **"Neutral," "independent," "we never take money from funeral homes."**
   These are true today and enforced in code. Keep the code tripwires as
   the substantiation record.
3. **Savings figures and "fair price" labels.** Guardrail #4 (n>5,
   significance, methodology) is the CPPA substantiation standard in
   practice. The claims register (`docs/SITE_AUDIT_2026-07/A3-CLAIMS-REGISTER.md`)
   is the evidence file.
4. **Who pays.** § 28-3904(f) makes it a violation to "fail to state a
   material fact if such failure tends to mislead." If a hospice pays for
   the family's experience and the flow says only "free, independent," a
   DC plaintiff could argue the payer is material. This sharpens counsel
   packet Q9 (disclosure) from "may we" toward "must we." **[COUNSEL]**

## 7. Hospice side

- DC hospices are licensed under the Health-Care and Community Residence
  Facility, Hospice and Home Care Licensure Act (D.C. Code § 44-501 et
  seq.). **[SECONDARY]** The bereavement specifics in the DC hospice rules
  were **[NOT RETRIEVED]**. The federal 418.64(d) framing applies to DC
  hospices regardless.
- **D.C. Code § 4-802(c)–(d) (DC Medicaid):** no one may solicit, accept,
  or agree to accept remuneration for referring a recipient to a provider
  of items or services payable under the DC Medicaid program, or for
  recommending the purchase of such items. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT via
  summary]** DC Medicaid covers hospice, so the AKS memo's analysis
  transfers.

## 8. Registration and tax

1. **Foreign registration** of the entity with DLCP Corporations if doing
   business in DC (registered through CorpOnline). A trade name is
   registered the same way unless the legal name already is "Honest Funeral
   Co." **[SECONDARY]**
2. **Basic Business License:** a "General Business License" is the category
   for businesses "not regulated by an existing … board or commission." A
   home-based DC business needs a **Home Occupation Permit** in place of a
   Certificate of Occupancy. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT: dlcp.dc.gov]** Clean Hands
   certification is part of licensing.
3. **Sales tax on the hospice subscription:** DC taxes SaaS as a digital
   good / data processing service. The rate is **6% through 2026-09-30 and
   7% from 2026-10-01** (Office of Tax and Revenue notice). Economic nexus
   is $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions a year, and physical presence
   (a DC-resident founder) creates nexus regardless. **[SECONDARY + OTR
   notice via summary]** Nonprofit hospices may present DC exemption
   certificates. **[CPA]**
4. **No Board of Funeral Directors license** is available to the platform,
   or needed.

We found no comprehensive DC consumer-privacy statute in force comparable

et seq.). **[SECONDARY]**

## 10. Home-level claims: the anti-SLAPP Act

D.C. Code § 16-5501 et seq. provides a special motion to dismiss for claims
"aris[ing] from an act in furtherance of the right of advocacy on issues of
public interest." On a prima facie showing, the plaintiff must show the
claim is **likely to succeed on the merits** (§ 16-5502(b); *Competitive
Enterprise Institute v. Mann*, 150 A.3d 1213 (D.C. 2016)). The Act does not
apply in federal court (*Abbas v. Foreign Policy Group*, 783 F.3d 1328
(D.C. Cir. 2015)). Home-level price flags on consumer funeral pricing are a
strong "public interest" fit, especially with the District's own history of
publishing the same kind of information.

## 11. Enforcement reality

The District's enforcement history in this sector runs **against homes**
(the 2017 survey and warning letters, the Austin Royster settlement, the
online-price mandate), never against price publishers. The realistic DC
risk is a CPPA plaintiff testing our marketing claims, not a licensing
action.
