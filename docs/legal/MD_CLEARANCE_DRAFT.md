## 1. Question and position

May Honest Funeral Co. run its free family service and its hospice-paid
bereavement program for Maryland families without a license under Title 7
of the Health Occupations Article?

**Position: yes.** Maryland's definitions of licensed practice are built on
operating an establishment, preparing a body, or "for compensation, to
arrange for or make final disposition of a dead human body." The platform
operates no establishment, prepares nothing, and arranges no disposition.
The only compensation it receives is a flat institutional fee for
bereavement support, which is paid whether or not any family ever uses a
funeral home. There are two soft spots (§4). The any-person "assist in the
practice" clause is the one that matters, and it points at the same
selection and scheduling-relay behavior the Virginia memo says to redesign.
One product design therefore closes both.

## 2. The statutory framework

- **Health Occ. § 7-101 (definitions):**
  - "Practice funeral direction" means: (i) to operate a funeral
    establishment; (ii) **for compensation**, to prepare a dead human body
    for disposition; or (iii) **for compensation, to arrange for or make
    final disposition of a dead human body.** **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]**
  - "Practice mortuary science" covers the same ground plus embalming.
    **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT, partial; confirm the full text]**
- **§ 7-501 (any person):** a person "may not practice, attempt to
  practice, offer to practice, or **assist in the practice of** mortuary
  science" in Maryland unless licensed by the Board. **[SECONDARY via
  summary of the official Title 7 compilation]**
- **§ 7-502 (holding out):** unless authorized, a person "may not represent
  to the public by title, **description of services**, methods, procedures,
  or otherwise" that they are authorized to practice mortuary science.
  **[SECONDARY]**
- **Penalties:** the Board may issue a public cease-and-desist order or a
  civil fine of up to $5,000 per offense (§§ 7-508/-509), alongside
  criminal prosecution under § 7-501 and injunction actions (§ 7-316.1).
  **[SECONDARY; confirm the sections]**
- **§ 7-316 (licensee discipline):** among the grounds, the licensee
  "**solicits mortuary science business, either personally or by an agent,
  from a dying individual or the relatives of a dying individual**."
  **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]** The full list, including any ground on paying for
  referrals, was **[NOT RETRIEVED]**. Pull it.
- **COMAR 10.29.12.04 (solicitation):** a licensee may not engage in
  solicitation (in person, by phone, or by direct mail) "which amounts to
  fraud, undue influence, intimidation, or overreaching." "A licensee shall
  be accountable under this regulation if the licensee uses an agent or
  partnership to implement actions prohibited by this regulation."
  **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT via summary]**
- **Health-Gen. § 5-509 (right of disposition):** without a decedent's
  document, the right "to arrange for the final disposition" passes in
  priority order: surviving spouse or domestic partner, adult children,
  parents, adult siblings, **persons acting as representatives under a
  signed authorization**, guardians, and others. An "authorizing agent" is
  the individual with legal authority to arrange for and make decisions
  about final disposition. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT via summary]**
- **Preneed:** a Maryland preneed contract is "an agreement between a buyer
  and a licensed funeral director, licensed mortician, or surviving
  spouse," as described in 98 OAG 003. **[SECONDARY]** We found **no**
  Maryland analog to Virginia's any-person ban on "the business of preneed
  funeral planning." **[NOT FOUND; COUNSEL confirm, including the
  Business Regulation Title 5 rules for sellers of burial goods under the
  Office of Cemetery Oversight]**
- **2026 change:** Chapters 187/188 of 2026 (SB 413 / HB 698, signed
  2026-04-28) renamed the Board the **State Board of Morticians, Funeral
  Directors, and Crematories**. They also moved crematory and
  natural-organic-reduction oversight from the Office of Cemetery Oversight
  to the Board, effective 2026-07-01. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]** Nothing in
  those chapters touches information services.

## 3. Why the platform sits outside the licensed practice

1. **Both elements of clause (iii) are missing.** The platform does not
   "arrange for or make final disposition" of any body. The family arranges
   with the home, and the family is the § 5-509 authorizing agent. Nor is
   the platform paid "for" arranging a disposition. The hospice's flat
   bereavement-program fee is owed whether or not any family ever chooses a
   home, uses the quote tools, or has a funeral at all. This is why the
   services agreement's flat, census-tier, non-volume fee (AKS memo) also
   matters for Maryland licensure. **A per-family or per-funeral fee would
   weaken the "not for compensation" leg.** Record that in the fee
   discussion (counsel packet Q2).
2. **"Assist in the practice" means assisting the licensee's work.** Read
   with § 7-501's neighbors ("practice, attempt to practice, offer to
   practice"), "assist" reaches the unlicensed helper inside the practice:
   the embalming assistant or the unsupervised arranger. It does not reach
   the consumer's own adviser across the table. Reading it otherwise would
   reach the Funeral Consumers Alliance of Maryland & Environs, clergy, and
   hospice social workers. Lenity applies to a criminal statute.
3. **Maryland tolerates price publication.** FCAME publishes a statewide
   mortuary price survey covering Maryland, Delaware, and DC funeral
   establishments (latest 2023). It collects prices from general price
   lists or by phone. Consumers' Checkbook publishes home-level prices for
   Montgomery and Prince George's homes. No enforcement against either was
   found. **[SECONDARY]**
4. **The family is the authorizing agent** (§ 5-509) and signs everything
   directly.

## 4. The soft spots

### 4.1 "Assist in the practice" (§ 7-501) and the selection/scheduling relay

This is the same behavior as Virginia soft spot 4.1. Today the platform
tells a home the family "selected your firm … at the price you quoted" and
relays the scheduling of the arrangement meeting. A regulator could call
conveying a selection and brokering the arrangement conference "assisting"
the home's arrangement of a disposition. **The Virginia redesign closes
this as well:** a family-voice selection message, no price acceptance, and
no scheduling relay. **[COUNSEL: confirm the redesigned flow is not
"assisting."]**

### 4.2 § 7-502 "description of services" (holding out)

Maryland's holding-out clause reaches a "description of services," not only
titles. Copy such as "we help schedule the in-person arrangement meeting" or
"your advocate for funeral arrangements" describes services close to the
licensed ones. Use "we help you compare prices and prepare questions; your
family makes all arrangements directly with the funeral home." Keep the
persistent disclaimer. **[COUNSEL: bless the copy set.]**

### 4.3 Watch item: "dying individual" solicitation by an agent

§ 7-316 disciplines a licensee who solicits "by an agent, from a dying
individual or the relatives of a dying individual." That is hospice
territory, pre-death. Two facts keep us out of it: the platform is **never
any home's agent**, and no home ever pays or directs us. It also means a
home that tried to use the platform to reach hospice families would be
risking its license. That is one more reason guardrail #1 and the
family-initiated rule are not negotiable here.

## 5. Right of disposition: never be the "representative under signed authorization"

§ 5-509's priority list includes "persons acting as representatives under
signed authorization." The platform must **never** accept appointment as a
family's representative for disposition decisions, even if a family offers.
Our "Authorization reference" in outreach emails authorizes a
**price-information request**, not disposition decisions. Say exactly that
in the authorization text, and write it into the terms. **[COUNSEL:
authorization-language review.]**

## 6. Consumer protection (MCPA)

- The Maryland Consumer Protection Act (Com. Law Title 13) bans unfair or
  deceptive trade practices in the offer of "consumer services." Its
  § 13-104 exemptions cover listed professions (lawyers, accountants,
  physicians, and others). Funeral services and information services are
  not on the list. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT]** Whether a free service is
  "offered for sale" is arguable. Our honesty duties are the same either
  way. Private actions are available (§ 13-408). **[SECONDARY]**

## 7. Hospice side

- **COMAR 10.07.21 (hospice care programs):** a written bereavement plan is
  initiated at admission, based on an assessment of the family's needs.
  Bereavement services run at least one year after death. **[plan at
  admission: OFFICIAL-EXTRACT; one-year span: SECONDARY]** Same procurement
  framing as Virginia: the hospice is buying something its state license
  requires.
- **Certificate of need (COMAR 10.24.13):** the Maryland Health Care
  Commission reviews new general hospice programs and expansions into new
  jurisdictions, jurisdiction by jurisdiction. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT via
  summary]** Market consequence: each county has a short, knowable list of
  authorized general hospices, which makes the Montgomery and Prince
  George's target list small and complete.
- **Crim. Law § 8-511 (state Medicaid kickback):** no person may solicit,
  offer, make, or receive a kickback or bribe connected to items or
  services paid in whole or part under a State health plan. It is a felony
  (up to 5 years and $100,000) when the kickback exceeds $1,500, a
  misdemeanor below that, and carries civil liability up to three times the
  kickback. **[OFFICIAL-EXTRACT via summary]** Maryland Medicaid pays for
  hospice, so the AKS memo's inducement analysis transfers. **[COUNSEL:
  confirm, and flag any Maryland all-payer analog.]**

## 8. Privacy: MODPA is the strictest law in the market

The **Maryland Online Data Privacy Act** took effect 2025-10-01, with
enforcement from 2026-04-01. **[SECONDARY via multiple firm alerts]**
- **Thresholds:** personal data of at least 35,000 Maryland consumers
  (excluding data used only for payments), **or** at least 10,000
  consumers plus 20% or more of revenue from selling personal data. These
  are much lower than Virginia's.
- **Nonprofits are covered.** Unlike most states, the exemptions are
  narrow.
- **Sensitive data**, which includes consumer health data, may be processed
  only when **strictly necessary** to provide the service the consumer
  requested. Its sale is banned outright.
- **Why this matters for the moat:** the business plan's moat is a
  proprietary outcomes dataset. Once MODPA applies (35,000 Maryland users
  is reachable for a free tool with DMV reach), processing a Maryland
  family's personal data for the dataset has to fit "strictly necessary"
  (sensitive) or "reasonably necessary and proportionate" (ordinary). That
  argues for building the outcomes dataset on **de-identified** records
  from day one, which MODPA excludes from "personal data." **[COUNSEL: the
  de-identification standard, and whether grief check-ins or hospice
  status are consumer health data under MODPA.]**

## 9. Registration and tax

1. **Foreign qualification** with the State Department of Assessments and
   Taxation (SDAT) if doing business in Maryland. A founder living and
   working here almost certainly is. Every entity registered with SDAT files
   an annual report (and a personal property return where applicable) with
   a filing fee. **[SECONDARY; confirm the current fee]**
2. **Trade name:** register "Honest Funeral Co." with SDAT if the legal
   entity's name differs. It becomes moot once the Delaware corporation
   carries the name. **[SECONDARY]**
3. **Sales tax on the hospice subscription (the real item):** Chapter 604
   of 2025 imposed a **3%** sales and use tax, effective 2025-07-01, on data
   and IT services and software publishing (NAICS 518, 519, 5415, 5132).
   SaaS sold for use in an enterprise system is taxed at 3%. SaaS for
   individual use is taxed at the general 6%. The old SaaS exemption was
   repealed. **[SECONDARY via Comptroller Technical Bulletin No. 56
   coverage]** A partner-portal subscription sold to a hospice is most
   likely 3%. Many Maryland hospices are nonprofits holding sales-and-use-
   tax exemption certificates. Collect the certificate at signature.
   **[CPA: confirm the classification of a "bereavement support program"
   invoice; register with the Comptroller if there is physical nexus.]**
4. **No Board license** is available to the platform, or needed.

## 11. Enforcement reality

We found no Board or Attorney General action against a price publisher or
navigation platform. FCAME's survey and Checkbook's ratings operate openly.
The Board's disciplinary machinery (COMAR 10.29.10 monetary penalties) is
aimed at licensees, and the realistic trigger is again a licensee
complaint about our outreach.
