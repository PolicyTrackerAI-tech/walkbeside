> 2026 General Session) and the R156-9 rule compilation. This is the #1

## 1. Question and position

May Honest Funeral Co. operate its free family service (funeral-price
education, comparison, itemized-quote support, negotiation coaching) and its
hospice-paid program in Utah without a license under the Funeral Services
Licensing Act (Utah Code Title 58, Chapter 9)?

**Position: yes.** The "practice of funeral service" definition
(§ 58-9-102(25)) is a closed list anchored in custody of remains,
preparation and disposition acts, conducting ceremonies, selling funeral
merchandise, and preneed selling. The platform does none of these: it takes
no custody, prepares nothing, conducts nothing, sells nothing, handles no
funds, and forms no contracts; the family holds the statutory right and duty
to arrange (§ 58-9-602) and signs everything directly. Utah's own exemptions
show a permissive posture (anyone may sell caskets unlicensed with 90-day
delivery, § 58-9-305(3); "any other recognized individual" may perform
services, § 58-9-305(4)), the licensee-conduct rules align with rather than
strain the model, and no enforcement action against a price publisher or
navigation platform was found in Utah history. Two clauses need counsel's
construction before this position is treated as settled (§4).

## 2. The statutory framework (verbatim anchors)

- **§ 58-9-301:** "A license is required to engage in the practice of
  funeral service, except as specifically provided in Sections 58-1-307 and
  58-9-305." Four license classes only: director, intern, establishment,
  preneed sales agent. There is no license category a platform could hold.
- **§ 58-9-102(25):** "Practice of funeral service" means: (a) supervising
  receipt of custody and transportation of a body; (b) entering into a
  contract to provide professional services regulated under this chapter;
  (c) embalming or preparing a body; (d) supervising the arrangement or
  conduct of a funeral, graveside, or memorial service; (e) cremation and
  similar; (f) supervising the arrangement of a disposition; (g)
  **"facilitating: (i) a disposition; or (ii) a direct disposition"**; (h)
  supervising the sale of funeral merchandise by an establishment; (i)
  managing an establishment; (j) supervising preneed sales; (k) contracting
  with or employing preneed sellers.
- **§ 58-9-305 (exemptions), highlights:** unlicensed sale of funeral
  merchandise/headstones with delivery within 90 days; performance of
  funeral, graveside, or memorial services by clergy, family, "or any other
  recognized individual"; nonprofessional tasks not requiring independent
  professional judgment.
- **§ 58-9-501 (unlawful conduct, reaches any person):** invasive
  preparation of bodies unlicensed; use of the four protected titles
  ("funeral service director," "funeral service intern," "preneed funeral
  service sales agent," "funeral service establishment"); providing or
  selling **preneed** arrangements unlicensed. Class A misdemeanor
  (§ 58-9-503). Price education and navigation appear nowhere in the list.
- **§ 58-9-502 (unprofessional conduct, binds licensees only per
  § 58-1-501(2)(a)):** (1)(b) knowingly soliciting at-need or
  imminent-death sales; (1)(c) paying "a commission or anything of value to
  secure deceased human remains for a funeral service or disposition to …
  a hospice; a hospice employee; … or any other third party." And the
  express carve-outs at (2): general advertising to the public at large,
  and **"responding to a notification of death from … a hospice; a hospice
  employee; … or any authorized representative of the family."**
- **§ 58-9-602:** the right and duty to control disposition and
  "arrangements for funeral goods and services" vests in the family.
- **R156-9 (DOPL rule):** adds nothing expanding the practice definition;
  R156-9-502(6) bars licensee commissions for body procurement;
  R156-9-502(13) bars licensees exercising undue influence causing
  purchases "beyond those the consumer desires or needs."

## 3. Why the platform sits outside the licensed practice

1. Every operative clause of (25) is a custody, preparation, ceremony,
   selling, or supervising function. The platform performs none. Clause (b)
   is circular: it reaches contracts to provide *regulated* services, and a
   subscription for price education is not one.
2. § 58-9-501 shows exactly what the legislature criminalizes for
   non-licensees, and information services are absent. Rule of lenity
   applies to a misdemeanor statute.
3. The licensing scheme is physically impossible for a platform to satisfy
   (an establishment license requires an embalming room and a refrigeration
   room, § 58-9-302(3)), signaling it was never aimed at one.
4. A state that lets an unlicensed retailer sell the casket (§ 58-9-305(3))
   can hardly treat telling a family what caskets cost as licensed practice.
5. The family, not the platform, is the statutory arranger (§ 58-9-602);
   the platform advises the rights-holder, who signs everything directly.
6. Utah policy affirmatively embraces comparative price publication (the
   state runs its own health-price comparison site) and conscripts funeral
   licensees into FTC Funeral Rule disclosure (§ 58-9-505). Truthful price
   information also carries First Amendment weight.

## 4. The two soft spots (buy counsel's answer on exactly these)

**Soft spot 1: "facilitating a disposition," § 58-9-102(25)(g).** Undefined
in the chapter and the rule; no Utah case, AG opinion, or DOPL guidance
construes it. A motivated regulator's first citation. Counters: noscitur a
sociis (it sits among hands-on effectuation roles); a literal
information-reading would swallow § 58-9-305(4) and criminalize clergy,
hospice social workers, and newspapers publishing price surveys; lenity.
**[COUNSEL: construction opinion; and whether to seek informal DOPL comfort
or a declaratory order under § 63G-4-503, or strategically not to ask.]**

**Soft spot 2: the quote-request feature.** The negotiate flow's outreach,
where itemized-quote requests go to funeral homes for a named at-need
family, is the platform's closest approach to "arranging/facilitating."
The safe shape, already largely designed in: the family initiates and
authorizes each contact in writing in-product; the message is an
information request invoking disclosures the home already owes any caller
under the FTC Funeral Rule and § 58-9-505; the platform never conveys
acceptance, never forms a contract, and every agreement is executed
family-to-home directly. **[COUNSEL: confirm whether platform-transmitted,
family-authorized sends suffice, or whether sends should issue under the
family's identity; specify the preferred authorization/agency language.]**

**Related product requirements this analysis imposes (all already true or
trivially enforceable):** no contract formation in-platform ever; any
pre-death documentation of a family's tentative selections labeled
non-binding (a preneed arrangement is any agreement "sold in advance of the
death," § 58-9-102(26), and unlicensed preneed selling is a crime for any
person, § 58-9-501(4)); prominent persistent disclaimers ("we are not a
funeral home or funeral director; we do not arrange funerals or handle
remains; your family contracts directly with the funeral home").

## 5. Solicitation, steering, and referral fees: the design maps cleanly

- The at-need solicitation ban binds licensees, and the platform's
  family-initiated activation keeps it outside even an aggressive reading.
  § 58-9-502(2) expressly blesses the channel shape the model uses:
  responding to a notification from a hospice or the family's authorized
  representative is NOT solicitation.
- The referral-fee rules prohibit the configuration guardrail #1 already
  forbids: anyone paying anything of value, directly or indirectly, to
  secure bodies, including payments involving hospices. The platform's
  economics (hospice pays the platform a flat fee; funeral homes never pay
  anything; no per-referral economics anywhere) sit on the correct side.
  One caution with teeth: accepting anything of value from a funeral home
  (featured placement, listings, success fees) could put the paying home in
  violation of § 58-9-502(1)(c)/R156-9-502(6) and expose the platform to
  aiding-and-abetting liability under § 58-1-501(1)(g), which reaches any
  person. Guardrail #1 is therefore also a Utah-law compliance requirement,
  not just brand law.
- Utah imposes no neutrality duty on intermediaries and has no referral-
  platform statute (it has no senior-placement-agency law either, unlike
  AZ/CO/WA). Neutrality remains compelled by our own claims (CSPA honesty:
  if we say neutral, be neutral) and by the federal payment-blind-directory
  analysis.

## 6. Name and holding-out

"Honest Funeral Co." uses none of the four protected titles in
§ 58-9-501(2). The residual theory is § 58-1-501(1)(a) (representing
oneself as practicing a licensed profession); mitigation is the disclaimer
set in §4, displayed persistently. **[COUNSEL: bless name + disclaimer text

## 7. Registration obligations found

1. **The d/b/a is mandatory, not optional.** Utah Code § 42-2-5 requires a
   person transacting business under an assumed name to file the assumed-
   name certificate with the Division of Corporations **within 30 days of
   commencing business under the name**, and § 42-2-10 bars maintaining any
   action in Utah courts until compliant (plus late fees). The whole
   chapter is recodified effective 10/1/2026 (2026 GS ch. 93; same
   substance, new citations). Consequence: register "Honest Funeral Co."
   on the LLC now; the future Delaware corporation carries the name as its
   legal name and needs no d/b/a.
2. No DOPL license category exists that the platform could or must hold.
3. No Utah Division of Consumer Protection registration applies to a free
   consumer-information service (telemarketing rules would need a re-check
   only if outbound phone contact of consumers ever begins; the
   family-initiated design does not implicate them).

## 8. Consumer-protection overlay (CSPA)

The Utah Consumer Sales Practices Act (§§ 13-11-1 et seq.) likely reaches
the platform as a "supplier" ("broker … whether or not the person deals
directly with the consumer") even though families pay nothing. Duties are
the ones the product already enforces as guardrail #4: no deceptive price
claims, no false neutrality or affiliation claims, substantiated
comparisons. Note for counsel: Chapter 442 of the 2025 session amended the
CSPA (scienter language, disgorgement remedy) and further provisions take
effect **October 1, 2026**; pull the incoming text before finalizing.
**[COUNSEL]**

## 9. Enforcement reality

No Utah DOPL or AG action against a funeral price publisher, comparison
site, or navigation platform was found. DOPL enforcement runs against
licensees (example: a 2023 license suspension over mishandled remains) with
roughly 11 investigators statewide. The FTC's 2024 Funeral Rule sweep sent
39 warning letters nationally; no Utah home appears in the published list.
